# Data Story: Where Does Growth Come From?

**Audience:** business stakeholders (sales and operations leads)
**Source:** classicmodels sales data, 320 orders, Jan 2003 – May 2005

## 1. Objective
Our sales business made $9.37M from 320 orders. Leadership wants to know where revenue comes from over the year, which products and customers matter most, and which finding is strong enough to plan around.

## 2. Analysis

**Revenue is seasonal.** Looking at the two full years (2003–2004), January to June is flat at roughly $180–210K a month. Revenue then climbs from July, and October to December brings **48% of yearly revenue** from 45% of orders. November alone is 26%, about 5 times June.

**Two product lines carry the business.** Classic Cars (40%) and Vintage Cars (19%) are **59% of revenue**. Margins are similar everywhere (35–42%), so selling more of the big lines matters more than shifting the mix. Trains are the thin spot at $179K.

**Customers come back, but a few are very large.** 62 of the 73 customers acquired in 2003 ordered again in 2004 (**85%**), and 97 of 98 active customers have ordered more than once. The top 10 customers make **28%** of revenue, and the top two alone make 15%. The 2004 cohort shows 48% so far, but it has had less time to reorder (data ends May 2005).

**One finding is tested statistically.** We asked whether the average value of Q4 orders is different from orders in the rest of the year. A Welch t-test gives +$3,869 per order (+13.9%), p = 0.036, 95% CI +$263 to +$7,475. The p-value is below 0.05, so we reject the null hypothesis, but only by a little: the evidence is moderate, not certain.

## 3. Conclusions
1. Revenue is highly seasonal: Q4 is the quarter that makes the year.
2. Customers are loyal, but revenue is concentrated in a few accounts.
3. Q4 orders are probably a little larger, but the main driver of Q4 is order volume, not order size.

## 4. Call to action
1. **Prepare for Q4 by September.** Stock and staff for a quarter that brings 48% of revenue, especially Classic and Vintage Cars.
2. **Fill the Jan–Sep gap.** Monthly revenue in these months is under half of November's. Test promotions or early-order discounts.
3. **Protect the 2004 cohort.** Only 12 of 25 have reordered so far. Follow up, and reactivate the 24 customers who have never ordered.
4. **Reduce concentration.** Two customers produce 15% of revenue. Grow mid-sized accounts so the loss of one cannot hurt.
